04

Connecticut · 8-30g Board

Gold Coast + Stamford/New Haven — real CT CAMA data with zoning-override flagging
The play: in towns under 10% affordable (Greenwich 5.3%, Westport 3.5%, Ridgefield, New Canaan, Darien), CGS 8-30g lets a 30%-affordable set-aside override local zoning denial — so estate-zoned vacant land becomes developable. Units are at the density that has actually been approved or settled in each town (Greenwich 6/acre, Darien/New Canaan/Westport/Ridgefield 8, Stamford/New Haven 20), capped at 40 units; the statute's ceiling is higher but backcountry sites have no sewer and multi-year appeals. Max offer is the residual land value with 30% of units sold at the deed-restricted price. Stamford/New Haven are 8-30g-exempt. Confirm sewer, wetlands and the town's current moratorium status before pursuing.

05

New Jersey · Redevelopment Board

Bergen/Hudson Gold Coast + GWB corridor — real MOD-IV parcel data
The play: NJ's lever is the 40A:20 long-term tax exemption PILOT — once a site is in a municipal "Area in Need of Redevelopment," the town can convey it directly (no bid) with a ~30-year PILOT (~10-15% of revenue vs. full taxes). This board leads with vacant + commercial/industrial (class 4A/4B) parcels — the ones that become redevelopment sites. Max offer is the residual land value at the town's assumed density (capped at 150 units); the asking proxy is the higher of assessed value and a published $/acre land floor, because NJ assessed values run below market by a town-specific ratio. Verify redevelopment designation on the municipal map before pursuing; the PILOT is the whole deal.

City-Owned Sites · RFP & Fast Track Pipeline

HPD · DCAS · EDC lots the engine found — these transfer by RFP, never by private sale
How this actually works in 2026. City land goes to developers through HPD/EDC RFPs and, since March 2026, HPD's Neighborhood Builders Fast Track: a rolling RFQ that pre-qualifies developers for small city-owned sites without a full RFP. The catch: the qualified entity must be a nonprofit or a certified M/WBE, and must hold at least 51% of the managing ownership interest in any joint venture. The practical structure for a non-M/WBE principal is a JV where the nonprofit or M/WBE partner holds 51%, you hold 49% and the GC contract. The sites below are the ones to watch for RFPs and to raise with the Council member and community board; the checklist is what the Fast Track submission needs.

FAST TRACKNeighborhood Builders Fast Track RFQ — what it takesHPD page ↗

01 · ELIGIBILITY

Nonprofit organizations or NYC-certified M/WBEs only. In a JV the nonprofit/M/WBE must hold ≥ 51% of managing ownership. A non-M/WBE developer or GC can be the 49% partner and builder.

Your move: line up one nonprofit or M/WBE co-developer who already has an HPD-completed project. They qualify; you bring the site work, the GC and the equity.

The church angle: a congregation is a nonprofit. On the Deal Board, the Faith filter lists congregations that own underbuilt land; a ground-lease JV with the church as 51% partner is both a Fast Track-eligible team and a site, with no land purchase. Church sales and long leases need a board resolution plus Attorney General or court approval (Religious Corporations Law §12 / N-PCL 511), so start with the trustees and budget 6-12 months.

02 · SUBMISSION

  • Email NeighborhoodBuildersRFQ@hpd.nyc.gov stating intent to submit, then register to download the RFQ.
  • Form A-1 completeness checklist · Forms C-1/C-2 residential development experience · Form D portfolio management · Form E financial capacity · no adverse findings.
  • One PDF, single-page layouts, table of contents, every form complete.
  • Rolling intake; the initial qualified list is expected Fall 2026 and updated periodically.

03 · HOW SITES GET ASSIGNED

HPD picks from the Qualified Developer List for site-specific mini-competitions; being listed does not guarantee a site. Separately, HPD/EDC publish full RFPs (5+ per year under the LIFT program) and the LIFT tracker maps the 100+ city sites in the pipeline.

Open HPD RFPs ↗ · LIFT site tracker ↗ · EDC RFPs ↗

04 · MOVING A LOT INTO THE PIPELINE

A city lot that is not yet in HPD's inventory (DCAS surplus, an agency lot that looks unused) gets there politically: the Council member for the district, the community board's land-use committee and the Borough President's office can ask DCAS/HPD to designate it. Bring the zoning math from this board and a nonprofit partner's letter of interest. Expect 2-4 years; do it for sites you also want for the long run.

05 · WHAT HPD SCORES

Completed affordable units (Forms C-1/C-2), units under management and compliance record (Form D), liquidity and net worth of the sponsor (Form E), M/WBE and nonprofit participation, design and sustainability, and community ties. Two or three finished 485-x buildings on this board's private lots are the fastest way to score on experience.

06 · SUBSIDY STACK ON A CITY SITE

Land at nominal cost, HPD subsidy loan (ELLA / Mix & Match / Open Door term sheets), 4% LIHTC equity, HDC tax-exempt bonds, 100% affordable at a 60% AMI average. The engine's ELLA path on each site shows the gap or surplus at $185k/unit of city subsidy; developer fee is 10-15% of TDC and is the profit.
